# Hurlin, Lajaunie, Pull (2026), reverse stress testing of geopolitical risk

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.03983v1, "Reverse Stress Testing Geopolitical Risk in
Corporate Credit Portfolios: A Formal and Operational Framework", submitted 7 January 2026.
Only the abstract and the summary of the method were read, not the full paper.

## What it does
Works backward from a capital threshold. A scenario is built from macro variables plus a
geopolitical risk measure, mapped to default probabilities and loss severities, aggregated
through a latent-factor model to tail losses, and converted to a stressed CET1 ratio that
reflects both capital erosion and risk-weighted assets. The reverse step searches for the most
likely scenario that pushes CET1 below the requirement, posed as a constrained maximum
likelihood problem; the solution is a "design point".

## Ideas worth taking
- **Design point.** The single most probable scenario that breaches a given threshold, found
  by optimisation under a likelihood measure. This is a sharper answer than "a scenario that
  breaches", because it ranks breaching scenarios by plausibility.
- **Near-breach neighbours.** Reporting scenarios close to the breach supports sensitivity and
  governance review.
- **Threshold as the input.** Start from a limit or capital figure Novera already holds.

## Where it would land in Novera
A reverse stress mode in `novera.risk.stress`, with the threshold taken from an existing limit
(MR-006). The scenario space would be Novera's risk factors, not the paper's credit variables.

## What we do not take
- **The credit and CET1 model.** It targets corporate credit portfolios and bank capital
  ratios. Novera's stress is market risk on a revalued book.
- **The geopolitical risk factor.** No such factor exists in Novera's universe.
- **Any estimate or parameter.** To be read in full before any design work starts.
